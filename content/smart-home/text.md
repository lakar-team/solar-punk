2014
My interest of developing smart IOT products started around 2014 after witnessing TESLAs success in producing their first real world practical version of a self-driving autonomous car. Communications technology development in the construction sector is slower than in the manufacturing sector. Therefore, the development of autonomous housing will soon be practical in the market.
2015
Early investigations cantered around wired home components. A collection of lights, speakers, chargers can provide housing items such as tables, chairs and beds with electrical energy to perform various task that was traditionally not expected of such household furniture. However, the components were still rather expensive and the products created would not be at an interesting price point for the market.
2018
Smart household fittings such as switches and lights started to be available in the market with varying degree of effectiveness and practicality. Early smart switches required a neutral wire connection making it tricky and costly for installation in renovation projects.
2019
Development of Smart home products is picking up globally. China produced components begin to rival European products with decent quality at a fraction of the cost. Early players in the local Malaysian market start to bring in products in bulk from China providing warranties for the products sold locally. More products come with wireless capability, eliminating the need for costly wiring works.
2020
Products available still does not have 100% performance reliability. Early adopters of the products are Smart home hobbyist and tech savvy individuals. Wired smart homes still provide high end clients with better peace of mind and reliability. However future adaptability is very limited for wired smart home systems.
In order to perform real world test with newer, more affordable smart home components that was unproven, I offered free smart home upgrades to my clients. The free aspect of the experiment makes it more acceptable for casual smart home enthusiast. It also reduces the liability inherent in a cheaper and less reliable wireless smart home system.
A small percentage of the project cost was allocated to experiment with smart home products available in the Malaysian market.

TEST INSTALLATION, 2020
1st Condominium, Bukit Jalil
The first project was a trial of the effectiveness of the Google Home smart speaker to control an IR sensor within a single space. This enabled the control of the TV , Fan and Aircon remote by voice.
2nd Bungalow, Sungai Penchala
The next project was trying to link the voice control system with the wired lighting and fan switch. Motion sensors were used here. The space was also larger with two Fans, and two Airconditioning units. To avoid any issue with the smart home system, we partnered with https://www.explosoft.com.my/ to provide the parts warranty and installation support. The products were offered at a good price, however the Manufacturer limited the components adaptability with other 3rd party products.
3rd Condominium, Menjalara
This project provided more flexibility to experiment as it was a personal project. The basic methods needed to link the smart home was established in the earlier projects. However, this time products were sourced openly to enable long term adaptability. Additional smart home components could be added into the system when it became available. The zigbee wireless network was used to enable more components to join in the system without crowding the wifi network. Multiple of each component were used to cater to multiple spaces. However, vampire load drives up the electrical bill cost as opposed to a non-connected home.

LIMITATIONS
Network limit.
One limitation in the system is that the network begins to get crowded when more components are installed in the network causing signal delays or mixed signals. A lot of the components still rely heavily on the wifi network to connect to the smart home system. Wifi networks are often burdened by data load from regular internet usage.
Each wireless network has also a range limit and penetration limit when implemented in the built environment. The wireless network is also less stable. Certain components can be missing from the system for short periods of time causing the system to get confused on how to execute the desired command.
Intelligence limit.
Another issue with this smart home network system is that the system is not truly smart. Although the devices are connected to the web and is able to be controlled remotely, almost all of the instructions has to be given into the network by the user. Any automation programmed into the current system falls under the IFTTT information model. IFTTT stands for "If this than that". It is a set of precise instructions given to the network to trigger certain settings. This system lacks any real Artificial Intelligence to predict and learn from home usage trends. The IFTTT model is also limited due to the complex situations of household electrical usage patterns. The next generation of smart houses (as suggested by https://lifesmart.com.my/) with cloud based Machine learning AI systems will be able to learn, predict and execute the homes controls more appropriately. One suggestion to achieve this is through Microsoft AI builder.
