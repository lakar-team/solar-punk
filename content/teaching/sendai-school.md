## The presentation (fully documented from MIA presentation.pdf)

A 14-slide visual presentation for Japanese elementary school children, introducing Malaysia from scratch. All slides in Japanese with visual labels — built to be understood without prior knowledge.

Topics covered in sequence:
1. Malaysia flag + phonetic reading (マレーシア / まれーしあ)
2. Geography — distance from Japan (8 hours by plane; by car via continent = 20 days)
3. Climate — Malaysia is tropical / always hot; Japan has four seasons
4. Map of Southeast Asia — Malaysia's two parts (peninsular + Borneo)
5. Coast and village house (kampung) — traditional raised timber house
6. Sea — stilt houses over water (Borneo sea people) + coral reef diving
7. Old town — colonial shop-houses (Penang/Georgetown)
8. Kuala Lumpur — Petronas Towers, high-rise apartments, rooftop pools
9. National costumes — 5 ethnic groups: Malay, Chinese, Indian, Kadazan/Orang Asli, Iban; adults and children shown
10. Food — Cendol, Nasi Lemak, Roti Canai, Laksa, Char Kway Teow, Chicken Rice (with Japanese pronunciations)
11. School uniform — Malaysian children in primary school uniforms
12. School subjects — math, science, music, Bahasa Malaysia (in Japanese)
13. Traditional games — tutup botol (bottle caps), Gasing (spinning tops), Congkak (mancala)
14. Closing — Terima Kasih + ありがとうございます

Closing in both Malay and Japanese — the children learn their first words of Malay.

---

## Story angle

The presentation is entirely in Japanese. Adam didn't just show up and talk in English about Malaysia — he prepared a bilingual, visually rich slide deck designed for 6–12 year olds, covering geography, food, games, clothes, and school life. The final slide teaches them to say thank you in Malay.

This is patient, considered cultural diplomacy — not a lecture, but an introduction designed to make Malaysia feel close and familiar to children who have likely never thought about it.
